A window in a brick veneer wall looks simple from the street. Underneath, it is a small sequence of decisions about structure, water and finish, made in a particular order by several trades. Get one wrong and it shows up later as a crack, a stain or a leak.

That makes it a good detail to teach from, and the one on our homepage.

## Twelve layers, in the order they're built

We model the opening as twelve separate layers, so each can be shown alone, taken away, or put back:

1. External brick wall
2. Internal steel framing
3. Wall and cavity ties
4. Head lintel and sill flashing
5. Angled brickwork sill
6. Timber sill and head frame
7. Window frame
8. Window side jamb
9. Internal plasterboard lining
10. Sill and head jamb lining
11. Window glazing
12. Sill flashing and internal trim

Each layer carries a short description of its purpose and the standards that govern it: AS 3700 for the masonry, AS/NZS 2699.1 for the ties, AS/NZS 2904 for the flashings, AS 2047 and AS 1288 for the window and its glass, and so on.

## Why layers

Drawings show a detail all at once. A building doesn't happen all at once. Pulling the brick veneer away to see the ties and the cavity tray, or taking the window out to see the double studs that frame the opening, shows *why* the detail is drawn the way it is.

> What you can take apart, you can understand.

## Inspection points

On top of the layers sit inspection points: notes pinned to the exact place they apply. Weep holes over the head. The lintel's bearing. Why the brickwork carries no load at all. They're written by people who teach construction, so they explain the thinking, not just the name.
