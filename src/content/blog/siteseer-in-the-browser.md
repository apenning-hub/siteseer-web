A building only teaches anyone if they can get to it. For most of SiteSeer's life that meant a desktop build: install it on a lab computer or a Windows or macOS laptop, and walk in. That still works, and it is still the richest way to use SiteSeer. But it left people out: the learner on a bus, the site supervisor with a tablet, the cohort whose labs are booked out.

## What's new

SiteSeer now has **web versions for iOS and Android**. Open a link on a phone or tablet and the building is there, in the browser, with nothing to download or install.

That makes four ways in:

- **Computer labs.** The Unreal Engine build deploys to pool and lab computers, for the full-fidelity experience in a timetabled class.
- **Learning platforms.** SiteSeer embeds in the platforms courses already run on, from Canvas, Moodle and Blackboard at university to Daymap and SEQTA in schools.
- **Laptops.** It runs on the Windows and macOS machines people already own. No headset, no lab booking.
- **Phones and tablets.** The new web versions, wherever someone happens to be.

## Why it matters

The value of a virtual site is that it can be visited *repeatedly*: before a lecture, after a site visit, the night before an assessment, or at the slab edge before a pour. Every barrier between a person and the building cuts into that. Removing the install step on mobile removes one more.

> The best site visit is the one people can make again whenever they need to.
